Healthcare Multi-Agent Intelligence

Not a hospital map — a system against false availability

This architecture doesn't solve the problem of finding the nearest clinic. It solves the problem of time wasted on broken promises: the bed exists in the report, but there's no oxygen, no doctor, no drug, or no real readiness to admit the patient.

Core idea: in Indian healthcare, the crisis isn't a lack of directories — it's that families and doctors can't tell who's actually ready to help right now.

What broke before

  • Ghost beds: a bed shows on the dashboard, but in reality there's no oxygen or staff.
  • Manual updates: one officer updates a form with hours of lag.
  • No coordination: ambulance, doctor slot, and drug availability live in separate systems.
  • No memory of dishonesty: a hospital can promise one thing and face no consequences.

What this deck sells

  • Verify-All-4 instead of bed-only dashboards.
  • Book-All-4 instead of phone calls and manual coordination.
  • Living Trust instead of binary "available / not available".
Problem 01

Government bed dashboards have already lost

Postmortem research shows a repeating pattern: data goes stale, beds are counted without clinical readiness, patients travel for hours and are turned away on arrival.

Phantom beds

A bed shows as available, but the hospital has no working oxygen, no critical drugs, or no relevant doctor on shift.

Human-in-the-loop latency

Manual updates in a web form lag 12+ hours. Such a dashboard isn't just useless — it actively misroutes ambulances.

No queue, no commitment

Even if a bed is "available," nobody reserves the ambulance, doctor slot, and drugs as a single bundle.

Key numbers from the postmortem

  • Punjab audit: Tocilizumab stockout lasted 294 days.
  • Liquid oxygen tanks were purchased but never connected to the pipeline.
  • By 2024–2025 many state dashboards went dead, stale, or repurposed.

Implication: if we just show another list of beds, we repeat a proven failure. We have to sell clinical readiness, not "bed presence".

Problem 02

The truth gap in the data kills trust

The dataset isn't normalized: capabilities are described in free-form notes, claims contradict equipment logs, and a hospital's formal status doesn't guarantee operational readiness.

"Advanced Surgery" with no anesthesiologist

The system has to do more than extract claims — it must flag suspicious ones, so the score isn't blindly trusted.

"24/7 availability" with no night-shift staff

A phrase in a report doesn't equal operational availability in the moment.

Static lists without confidence

When the source is stale or indirect, showing a deterministic answer is more dangerous than honestly showing uncertainty.

Why multi-agent is the right answer

One agent extracts, a second validates, a third routes. This is architecture against false confidence — not orchestration for orchestration's sake.

What the system actually solves

  • Extracts capability signals from 10k unstructured records.
  • Flags contradictions and lowers Trust instead of hiding the problem.
  • Shows citations down to the specific line in the report.

Core UX principle: better to show 0.62 ± 0.11 and explain why than to lie with the word "available".

Problem → Solution

Every module exists because it fixes a specific failure

False beds

TrustScorer checks bed + oxygen + drug + specialist, not bed count, and computes overall Trust as the product of those factors.

No ground truth in Tier 2

Predictor + Voice fallback give a probabilistic estimate and a rare live call only where confidence is genuinely weak.

Text lies or contradicts itself

Validator Agent catches absurd claims and prevents the router from building decisions on hallucinations.

Even the right choice falls apart on execution

Atomic Booking reserves four resources in one transaction. If anything fails, it rolls everything back instead of leaving the family with half a confirmation.

Hospitals can promise and forget

Outcome Loop + Agent Reputation create an economy of honesty: lie via agent handshake, lose trust, drop in ranking.

NGOs see the map but not the gaps

Desert Dashboard shows capability gaps by PIN code and turns routing data into planning intelligence.

Solution 01

Verify-All-4 removes the most expensive type of error

The costliest error in this system isn't "choosing hospital #2 instead of #1" — it's sending a patient to a place that's only ready on paper. So we verify the four dependencies without which the care path collapses anyway.

Factor 1

Bed

A place to receive the patient now, not just formal capacity in a table.

Factor 2

Oxygen

A bed without a working oxygen pipeline may be physical, but not clinically viable.

Factor 3

Drug

The needed drug must be in stock, otherwise the transfer ends in delayed treatment.

Factor 4

Specialist

Even the right hospital is useless if the relevant specialist isn't on shift.

Result

Living Trust

The system returns probability + confidence interval + freshness, not fake precision.

Why this beats a bed dashboard

  • Reduces false positives in the most dangerous cases.
  • Shifts the conversation from "how many beds" to "is the care path ready".
  • Gives the judge and the user a clear mental model of the decision.

Pitch line: bed-only systems optimize for the visibility of capacity; we optimize for the probability of treatment actually starting.

Solution 02

The two-tier model solves partial coverage from day one

The most practical trap in any healthcare platform: waiting for every hospital to integrate. This system doesn't wait. It runs in two modes, preserving coverage from the start.

Tier 1: HAS-AGENT

The hospital's IntakeAgent answers yes/no queries from HMIS, pharmacy ERP, and staff schedule. Fresh, signed handshakes and the highest trust ceiling.

Tier 2: NO-AGENT

Predictor + extraction + crowd / outcome signals cover the market without deep integration. Coverage exists day one — not "after the enterprise rollout".

Without Tier 2

The system would become an elitist network of a few digitized hospitals and would lose the challenge on scale and social impact.

Without the Tier 1 incentive

Hospitals would have no reason to give quality signals. Here, onboarding earns a higher Trust ceiling and more patients.

What failure this fixes

  • We can't wait for full digitization across the country.
  • We can't tie UX to a single type of integration.
  • We need scale and a gradient of trust at the same time.

Strong framing: Tier 1 and Tier 2 aren't a compromise. They're a way to honestly say where we know and where we only estimate.

Solution 03

Atomic Booking closes the coordination collapse

Even the right recommendation is useless if the family then has to manually arrange bed, ambulance, doctor slot, and drug queue separately. Coordination has to be part of the product, not an external process.

What gets reserved in one transaction

Bed reservation, ambulance dispatch, doctor slot, and drug reservation commit as a single bundle in Delta ACID.

What happens on failure

If even one resource fails, the transaction rolls back fully and the system automatically suggests the next hospital, never creating partial promises.

What the world looks like without this

The family has a "confirmation" for a bed but no drug; the ambulance is en route but the doctor isn't there; the transfer technically started, clinically failed.

Why this is an especially strong demo moment

  • Shows the system doesn't just reason — it drives to operational action.
  • Sells the Databricks-native story through the Delta ACID transaction.
  • Clearly answers "and what happens after the recommendation?".

Slide line: we don't send the patient "somewhere to go". We reserve the entire path to care.

Solution 04

Outcome Loop turns errors into a training signal

Most healthcare registries decay because they never learn how the actual patient experience ended. Here, feedback two hours after routing keeps trust living and self-correcting.

The patient confirms reality

An SMS / WhatsApp question on bed, drug, and specialist gives ground truth without building heavy hospital-side telemetry everywhere.

The Predictor improves

For Tier 2, the outcome signal provides real labels — exactly what historical bed dashboards always lacked.

Agent Reputation punishes dishonesty

If a Tier 1 hospital lies through its IntakeAgent, its reputation and Trust ceiling drop automatically.

Without this, every system ages

No memory of failure, no mechanism for trust decay, no incentives for honesty. In a couple of months the dashboard becomes another beautiful but dishonest layer.

New value

  • The system doesn't just find a hospital — it measures its own truthfulness.
  • Every refusal or mismatch improves future ranking.
  • A rare case where the UX loop directly strengthens model quality.

Strong line: honest hospitals get more traffic; lying hospitals are pushed down by the algorithm itself.

Solution 05

The NGO dashboard solves invisible medical deserts

For NGOs and planners, the value isn't only where to take one patient right now. They need a map of chronic capability gaps: where dialysis, trauma, oncology, or real emergency readiness is missing.

From patient routing to planning intelligence

Trust-scored data is aggregated by PIN code and shows not just facility counts, but the real failures by specialty and readiness.

Actionable desert map

You can see districts where nominal hospitals exist, but 4-factor trust on critical fronts is systematically low.

What it looked like before

Planning was built on registries of buildings and beds. But a facility registry isn't a registry of actually working care pathways.

Why this matters for the challenge

  • Covers the Social Impact and Utility weight.
  • Makes the product useful not only in emergencies, but for policy and planning.
  • Translates technical architecture into clear public impact.

Core idea: we're not looking for the nearest hospital. We show where the healthcare system systematically falls short.

Tier 1 / Tier 2 architecture gives immediate coverage without waiting for the country to fully integrate.
